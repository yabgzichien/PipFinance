# Pip: UI design plan

Internal design document. The counterpart to `business-plan.md`: that one decides what Pip
means, this one decides what Pip looks like and what it puts in front of a user first.

Produced via a `/grill-me` interview, so every decision below was pressure-tested against a
measurement taken from this codebase before it was accepted. Where a decision was declined or
deferred, that is recorded too, with the reason.

Scope: the visual system and information architecture of the tracker shipping to Google Play on
`release/play-store`. The Stage 2 onboarding redesign is explicitly deferred (§7), by decision,
not by omission.

Brief this answers: *"attention spans are short, keep the UI clean and only highlight what is
critical."*

---

## 1. What the audit found

Measured on the tree at the time of writing, not estimated.

| Metric | Pip today | Target | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distinct `fontSize` values | **30** across 507 declarations | 5 | everywhere |
| Text at 13.5px or smaller | **61%** of all type | ~25% | everywhere |
| Font weights in use | 5 (400/500/600/700/800) | 2 | everywhere |
| `uiFont(700)` bold usages | **206** (41% of all type) | under 15% | everywhere |
| Distinct spacing values | **29**, 61% off any 4pt grid | 6 | everywhere |
| Raw `fontSize` inside `src/screens/` | **370** | 0 | vs 9 in `ui.tsx` |
| Blocks stacked on Home | up to 8, 12 tap targets | 5 | `DashboardScreen.tsx` |
| Routes from Home to Net Worth | **3** | 1 | nav, summary card, quick actions |
| `InfoButton` "?" affordances | 28, for 10 glossary terms | fewer screens needing them | 9 in `CashflowStructure.tsx` alone |

### The five findings

**1. Nothing is emphasised, because everything is.** This is the root cause of "busy", and it is
the top block of the table. When three-fifths of the type is caption-sized and two-fifths of it
is bold, a three-second glance returns nothing. Every other visual complaint is downstream of
this one.

**2. Home led with a streak and hid the money behind a choice.** The scroll order put the streak
card above the summary, and the summary opened with a segmented control, so the user had to
*pick* Cash flow or Net worth before a number appeared. Two candidate heroes means no hero.

**3. Navigation duplicated itself.** `QuickActions` offered Budget and Net Worth tiles directly
below a card that already segmented to Net Worth, on a screen whose tab bar already has a Net
Worth tab.

**4. The differentiator was buried and the privacy pillar was contradicted in-app.**
`business-plan.md` §3 names the twin pillar: screenshot the apps you already live in, and it
never leaves your phone. In the shipped app the screenshot row was third and collapsed, beneath
"Scan a receipt" (the claim `business-plan.md` §3 says a competitor already owns), and `AttachScreen.tsx:220`
disclosed in 12.5px grey that screenshots are transmitted to a third-party AI provider. See §5.

**5. Dead assets, live need.** `SAMPLE_STATEMENTS` ships real Touch 'n Go, Maybank MAE and Grab
payout screenshots in the bundle, generated by `tools/demoKit`. The `showSamples` prop that
surfaces them defaults to false and is passed `true` from nowhere: leftovers from the deleted
judge tour. They are exactly the asset a first-run needs (§7).

### What was already right, and must not regress

- **The raised Add button in the tab bar.** Correct action, correct thumb zone, on screen from
  every primary tab. Do not move it back into the feed.
- **`tools/contrastAudit/audit.js`.** A mechanical build check that fails on sub-AA pairs, with
  every value in `theme.ts` carrying its measured ratio. The amber tint is 5% instead of 7%
  specifically because 7% broke the ratio. This is the discipline §4 extends to type and spacing.
- **`SavedScreen.tsx`.** The only screen in the app that already knows it is a peak moment.

## 2. Decisions taken

Seven, in the order they were settled. Each one's cost is recorded because each one has a real
cost that someone will want to renegotiate later.

| # | Decision | What it kills | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Home gets one adaptive hero number (§3) | the "pick your metric first" pattern | a rung rule to explain when the number changes |
| 2 | Cut Quick Actions, the summary toggle, and Safe Income from Home | three blocks, three duplicate routes | net worth loses its glanceable slot on Home |
| 3 | Streak stays a card, moves below the hero, drops the coverage metric | the second competing progress metric | weaker habit signal above the fold |
| 4 | 5 sizes, 2 weights, enforced by a build check (§4) | 25 sizes, 3 weights, freehand type | 507 call sites migrate; every screen grows 15-25% |
| 5 | Same audit governs spacing: 4/8/12/16/24/32 | 23 spacing values, the 61% off-grid rate | folded into the same migration |
| 6 | Privacy pillar reworded now, on-device OCR later (§5) | the false "never leaves your phone" headline | the strong claim is unavailable until the engineering lands |
| 7 | Onboarding redesign deferred; copy and Attach order fixed now (§7) | receipt-first misdirection at both entry points | the eight-step cold start survives until Stage 2 |

Decision 1 was reached against an initial preference for a fixed Net cash flow default with a
Settings override. That was declined for two reasons, both provable from the code:

- **Day-one red.** `net = received − spent` over the calendar month, painted `colorTheme.red`
  below zero. A first-time user scanning a Touch 'n Go or GrabPay screenshot has no salary in
  that image, so `received = 0` and the first number Pip ever shows is a large red negative.
  `business-plan.md` §2 describes this person as checking their balance "the way you check a wound."
- **The payday sawtooth.** Salaried, paid the 25th: the hero is red for roughly 24 of 30 days,
  and the swing is driven by the calendar rather than by anything the user did.

The Settings override survives (§3), but as an override, not as the mechanism. The person harmed
by a bad default churns in week one, before they ever open a Settings screen where this would sit
as section five of seven.

## 3. Home

```
Header            date · greeting · Pip
──────────────────────────────────────
HERO              adaptive number, Display 40
                  one line of context beneath
Where it goes     top 3 categories, bar + %
🔥 Streak          count + 7 dots
Needs you         at most one row
Budget
```

Five blocks, down from eight. Net worth is reachable one way: its tab.

### The adaptive hero

The rung is chosen from state the app already computes. No user configuration is required to
reach the right number.

| Rung | Condition | Hero | Context line |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | `incomeBaseline.method === 'none'` | `RM 340` spent this month | "so far in September" |
| 1 | income on record, `!hasBudget` | `+RM 1,240` net cash flow | "income − expenses · this month" |
| 2 | `hasBudget` | `RM 840` left to spend | "11 days left in September" |

Rules:

- **Rung 0 is never red.** Money spent is a fact, not a verdict, and it is the only rung a
  first-run user can be on. Style it in `ink`, not `red`.
- **Rungs 1 and 2 may go red**, because by then Pip knows enough for a negative number to mean
  something the user can act on.
- **Announce every promotion.** When the rung changes, show a one-time line under the hero:
  *"You set a budget. Home now shows what's left."* A number that changes without explanation
  reads as a bug.
- Each rung is a genuine upgrade earned by the user doing more, so the ladder doubles as a
  retention path rather than being pure defensive logic.

**Settings override.** A new "Home number" row in the existing Budget section of
`SettingsScreen.tsx`: Automatic (default) · Spent · Net cash flow · Left to spend. This is the
escape hatch for a user with an opinion, and it is expected to be used by very few of them. That
is the point: the default is the product.

### The other four blocks

- **Where it goes.** Top 3 categories with bar and percentage, "See all" to Breakdown. This is
  the tagline "Know your money" rendered as pixels, and it carries the highest information value
  per unit of screen on the app. It sits directly under the hero and must stay above the fold at
  375x812.
- **Streak.** Flame, count, seven day-dots. The "Covered 62/90 days" line is removed from Home:
  it is a data-confidence metric, in a different unit on a different timescale, with nothing on
  Home to explain what 90 means. It moves to Recap where it can be explained. The streak keeps
  feeding `syncStreakWidget`, so the habit lever in `business-plan.md` §4 is intact.
- **Needs you.** At most **one** row, priority-ordered: overdue commitment, then aged debt, then
  nothing. Today `owed`, `commitmentsDue` and Safe Income are three independently-eligible cards
  that can all render at once. One slot, one thing, or the block does not render.
- **Budget.** The allocation list when `hasBudget`, otherwise the set-a-budget CTA.

### Removed

`QuickActions` is deleted outright. Budget moves to a row inside the Where-it-goes block; Net
Worth keeps its tab. The summary card's segmented toggle is deleted. Safe Income relocates to the
Budget screen, where an irregular earner meets it during setup. The `incomeBaseline.ts` logic is
unit-tested and stays exactly as it is: only its placement changes.

Note while touching this file: the comment at `DashboardScreen.tsx:382` claims net worth is the
default view while line 408 sets cashflow. Both go away with the toggle.

## 4. The type and spacing system

The point is not the scale. The point is that **the scale is enforced by a build check**, because
a scale that is not mechanically enforced does not stay fixed. Pip is at 30 sizes today for
exactly one reason: nothing stops the 31st.

The precedent already exists in this repo. `tools/contrastAudit/audit.js` guards colour and
colour is the one genuinely disciplined part of the design system. Type and spacing get the same
treatment.

### Scale

| Primitive | Size / weight | Font | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `<Display>` | 40 / 700 | Space Grotesk for amounts, Hanken for words | one per screen, the hero |
| `<Title>` | 22 / 700 | Hanken | screen titles, card headlines |
| `<Body>` | 16 / 500 | Hanken | default; everything a user reads |
| `<Label>` | 13 / 700 | Hanken | eyebrows, meta, secondary rows |
| `<Caption>` | 11 / 500 | Hanken | timestamps, legal, genuinely rare |

Two weights only: **500 and 700**. That retires 400 (6 uses), 600 (157 uses) and 800 (9 uses).
The 600 tier is the one that will hurt: it collapses into 500 or 700 case by case, and the
default answer is 500. Bold that means something requires bold that is rare.

### Spacing

Six values: **4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32**. Replaces 29 values of which 61% sit off any 4pt grid.
`radius.sm/md/lg` (14/22/28) is left alone: it is already tokenised, only three values wide, and
renormalising it would change the product's silhouette for no measured gain.

### Enforcement

New `tools/typeAudit/audit.js`, run in CI alongside the contrast audit. It fails on:

- any raw `fontSize` outside `src/components/ui.tsx`
- any `fontFamily` not obtained via `uiFont()` / `numFont()`
- any padding, margin or gap literal not in the spacing scale

Exceptions go in an explicit allowlist in the audit config, so every exception is visible and
countable rather than invisible and unbounded.

### Migration, and the content budget nobody wants

Raising the floor from 11px to a readable 16px makes **every screen 15 to 25% longer**. Bigger
type is not a styling change, it is a content budget cut with a styling side effect. The cutting
done to Home in §3 has to happen on the other 24 screens too.

The rule that makes this concrete: **no screen may exceed 2.5 viewport heights of scroll at
375x812.** Home targets 1.5. `CashflowStructure.tsx`, carrying 9 of the app's 28 `InfoButton`
affordances, is the first screen that will fail this and the clearest signal that the screen
itself, not its explainers, is the problem.

Migrate screen by screen, one commit each, with the audit enabled through a shrinking allowlist
rather than switched on at the end. Roughly 507 declarations across screens and components. Each
screen's commit includes its content cut; a migration commit that only changes type has skipped
the actual work.

## 5. Capture and privacy

### What the code actually does

`groq.ts:178` and `gemini.ts:120` transmit the **entire screenshot**, base64-encoded, to Groq or
Google. Not extracted text. The image. A Maybank or Touch 'n Go screenshot contains the account
holder's name, a running balance, frequently a partial account number, and every merchant, amount
and date on screen. `handoff.md` separately flags that the API key is embedded client-side and
extractable.

`business-plan.md` §3 pillar 2 says *"It never leaves your phone."* That is false in the app's own
code, and the app's only disclosure of it is one 12.5px grey line below the fold.

### Resolution

The pillar gives way now; the engineering earns it back later.

**Honest wording, effective immediately:**

> No account. No cloud. Your records live on your phone.

This is true today: storage is local SQLite, there is no account and no sync. It gives up "never
leaves your phone" as a headline until on-device OCR ships.

**The disclosure becomes a designed trust moment,** shown once before the first scan, reopenable
from a persistent affordance, and not repeated on every scan:

```
Before scanning
────────────────────────────────────────
Pip sends this image to Groq to read it.
  ✓ Not stored by Groq
  ✓ Not stored by Pip anywhere but this phone
  ✓ No account, ever

  [ Read it ]        [ Type it instead ]
```

Competitors bury this. Showing it plainly, at the moment it matters, is a differentiator in its
own right against a market where Finory already markets on privacy. Transparency is the angle
available to Pip today; locality is the angle available after the roadmap item lands.

**Roadmap: on-device OCR.** ML Kit text recognition plus a local parser for Maybank, MAE, Touch
'n Go and GrabPay layouts. It makes the strong claim literally true and it dissolves two other
open problems at once: §5 of the business plan's unresolved free-tier scan cap, and the
extractable API key, both of which exist only because there is a network call. It is significant
work, brittle across bank app redesigns, and materially less accurate than a vision model. It is
not scheduled here.

**Play Store consequences, flagged rather than resolved (not legal advice):**

- The Data Safety declaration must state that financial info is collected **and transferred to a
  third party**. A listing claiming local-only beside a form declaring transfer is a mismatch
  Google's review looks for, and misdeclaration is a removal-class policy issue.
- No privacy policy exists anywhere in this repo. `business-plan.md` §8 already records this as a
  hard submission blocker. It remains one, and it is now also the document that has to describe
  the transfer above.

### Attach ordering

The differentiator goes first. `AttachScreen.tsx` becomes:

```
▾ Scan a statement or e-wallet      ← first, expanded by default
    [ Take a photo ]  [ From gallery ]
▸ Scan a receipt
▸ Enter it manually
▸ More ways to import               ← file import folded in here
```

Two moved JSX blocks and the `statementOpen` default flipped at `AttachScreen.tsx:59`. The
"ONE PURCHASE / MANY TRANSACTIONS AT ONCE" grouping is retired: it organised the screen by
conceptual tidiness rather than by what the business says wins, and it cost the differentiator
the top slot.

## 6. How the audience actually enjoys this

This section is a proposal derived from `business-plan.md` §4, not a decision taken in the
interview. It needs its own grilling before anyone writes final strings.

### The peak

Peak-end says a user remembers two moments: the most intense one, and the last one. Pip's peak is
unambiguous and already half-built: **you screenshot your bank app and fourteen transactions
appear, already categorised.** Nothing else in the product is close.

`SavedScreen.tsx` is where that lands, and it currently undersells it. The spring-pop and "All
sorted!" are right. But the payoff itself, *"14 transactions · RM 1,240 added"*, is set at 14.5px
sub-text under a mascot. Under the new scale that line is the `<Display>`:

```
              [ Pip, delighted ]

              14
              transactions · RM 1,240
              read in 6 seconds

  ✨ Pip learned 3 new merchants
     ZUS Coffee      → Food
     Grab            → Transport
     TNB             → Utilities
```

The elapsed time matters. "Read in 6 seconds" is the line that makes the difference against
manual entry legible, and it is the frame a user repeats to a friend.

### The end

The last impression is the Home glance on the way out. The adaptive hero does this job: one
number, one line, no decisions asked of a person who is closing the app.

### Pip's voice

`business-plan.md` §4 treats personality as a retention lever on par with the mechanism, citing Cleo's Roast Mode
and Duolingo's owl. In the shipped app it does not exist: Pip is a 40px decorative sprite and the
copy reads *"Plan income and allocate spend per category."*

The dosing rule from §4 holds, and the UI structure makes it concrete:

| Surface | Register | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Push notifications | passive-aggressive, occasional slang | high visibility, low frequency, the surface that gets screenshotted |
| Android widget | passive-aggressive, occasional slang | same |
| Empty and milestone states | passive-aggressive, self-aware | seen rarely, so a joke does not wear out |
| Streak and Saved screens | warm, light | the reward moment, not the punchline |
| Hero number, categorise, net worth, budget | **plain** | a joke every time you check your balance gets old by week two |

Concretely, the strings that change first, because they are the ones a new user meets:

- Home empty state, today *"No spending yet"* → something in §4's register.
- `SavedScreen` "All sorted!" → keep, it is already correct in tone.
- Budget CTA, today *"Plan income and allocate spend per category."* → plain, but human.

The half-life caveat from §4 applies and should be written into whatever voice guide follows:
slang dates fast, and Pip is safer being in on its own joke than performing one.

## 7. Not in scope

Recorded so nobody re-opens them by accident.

- **The onboarding redesign.** `plan.md` scopes the stub to Stage 1 and the real experience to
  Stage 2, and that line is held here deliberately. Two near-free pieces of it ship now because
  they contradict positioning already decided: the onboarding sentence (today
  *"Scan receipts, track income and expenses, and see your net worth in one place"*, which leads
  with the one claim `business-plan.md` §3 says not to compete on) becomes the tagline plus the real mechanism, and
  the Attach reorder in §5. The eight-step cold start survives until Stage 2, so the redesign
  still has a job.
- **Reviving `SAMPLE_STATEMENTS` as a guided first scan.** The strongest first-run option
  considered: onboarding *is* the first scan, run on a bundled Touch 'n Go sample, so the magic
  happens before the user is asked to trust anyone with real data. Declined for now. It stays the
  recommended Stage 2 shape, and the assets are already in the bundle. If it ships, the demo data
  must be visibly labelled as a demo and clearable in one tap; silently seeding fake transactions
  into a user's records would be dishonest.
- **On-device OCR** (§5). Roadmap, unscheduled.
- **The extractable client-side API key.** Deferred in `handoff.md`, unchanged here.
- **Final voice strings** (§6). Proposal only, needs its own pass.

## 8. Open risks

- **No privacy policy exists in the repo.** Already a hard Play Store blocker per
  `business-plan.md` §8. §5 raises the bar: it now also has to describe a third-party transfer of
  financial data. Nothing else in this document ships without it.
- **Data Safety declaration** must be written to match §5 of this document, not the original
  wording of `business-plan.md` §3.
- **The migration is 507 call sites** across 25 screens, each one carrying a content cut. It is
  the largest single piece of work here and the easiest to half-do. A screen migrated without a
  cut has skipped the point.
- **Free-tier AI scan cap is still unresolved** (`business-plan.md` §5). Unchanged by this
  document, but on-device OCR would dissolve it if it ever lands.
- **No store-listing screenshots exist for the current feature set.** Net Worth, Commitments and
  Split Bills, three of the supporting differentiators in `business-plan.md` §3, have never been captured. They should be
  captured *after* the type migration, not before, or they will be reshot.
- **Pip's voice is unbuilt** and `business-plan.md` §4 counts it as a retention lever on par with the mechanism. §6
  is a proposal, not a plan.
